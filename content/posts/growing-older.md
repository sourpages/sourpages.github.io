The thought of being alive or being born in this world is astonishing to me, especially when you consider the fact that the chances of being born are so low. There is absolutely no reason for us or anyone to stop exploring, wondering, getting lost in the beauty of the world or stop wondering why we are here, or who we are. Yet we do this, this possibly can’t only be laziness, more than that, it could be (I do indeed think that it is) our “comfort zone” in our minds which is certainly the result of our habits. We gradually stop being curious about the most astonishing things around us: the Earth, the Solar System, the stars, the existence of crabs, or trees, all of which took millions to billions of years. Yet, we are so lucky to be alive and be among them and be part of the history. It happens because we take this world and its existence, which I dare say includes humanity as well, for granted. A newborn who can’t yet communicate is probably always overwhelmed by his mere presence in the world, the surroundings, the cat in his house and what not even though he is experiencing repetitive things every time or at least regularly. This feeling of not taking anything for granted even though we see it everyday leads to curiosity and thus leads to finding new things.

This world is not beyond human reasoning, I believe. It is astonishing to think that every belief, every act of faith, every idea, and every piece of knowledge is a product of human mind. Reasoning is the only reliable means by which we distinguish knowledge from belief.

Did the ideas make the world as we see it or is it the world that has formed our ideas? It is debatable. Universally and eternally true ideas can be justified only through truth and knowledge, not faith. And truth is something that exists whether we exist or not. Somehow, human reasoning is indeed directly or indirectly the only means we uncover truth and knowledge. It just sits there waiting to be found. To conclude, human reasoning doesn’t give birth to truth, it is merely a process of finding it. And still we can’t rely on our senses to find something accurate and true, that is knowledge. Reasoning is the only path to knowledge, helped by our senses. I’m not sure if the surroundings as we see should affect knowledge. Although our senses are imperfect, we could not know most things without them. Astonishing how we create or find accurate ideas from imperfect media. For example, a sphere would still be a sphere even if humanity ceased to exist. We know what a sphere is even though we cannot distinguish between a slightly deformed ball and a perfectly spherical one using only our eyes or touch. This illustrates the nature of an idea: our senses present us with imperfect objects, yet through reasoning we arrive at the concept of a perfect sphere. A sphere is defined as the set of all points in three-dimensional space that are equidistant from a fixed point, its centre. The principles of geometry discovered and justified through reasoning help us validate the idea of a sphere.

It’s funny how we try to escape from this reality of this incredible world, and faith often sufficiently kills our curiousity to answer. Faith is a collection of ideas often built without sufficient evidence or inquiry. Funny how we keep pondering about things which echo in our small safe house that carry very little to no importance at all, overlooking the astonishment that surrounds us. 

**Do you not think that in this age of science we should focus more on finding knowledge than holding onto faith that has no scientific evidence? Faith, anyway, is just a progression to knowledge. And how would you give up this habit of ignorance, if it all? Apparently, acting astonished by everything isn't the answer. Simply not taking everything for granted can be one way.**
